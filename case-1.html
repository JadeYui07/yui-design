Track My Sleep Now

Optimizing Sleep Experience for Conversion and Retention

The Problem

Track My Sleep Now was struggling with high drop-off during onboarding and low premium conversion. Users weren't seeing enough immediate value to justify upgrading, and the existing design didn't align with how people actually think about sleep.

64% of users dropped off at the "setup soundscape" step — one of the app's most valuable features — because it was buried behind account creation and multiple setup screens. Users were being asked to commit before they'd experienced any value.

Research & Discovery

I started with a broad research phase to understand both users and the competitive landscape, before narrowing into the specific friction points causing drop-off.

1

Competitive Analysis

Audited 8 leading sleep apps — Calm, Sleep Cycle, Headspace — to identify onboarding patterns, value-first strategies, and paywall placement.

2

User Interviews

Conducted 12 interviews with current and lapsed users. Key theme: users want to hear a sound before committing to the app. Personalization felt generic and trust was low early in the flow.

3

Journey Mapping

Mapped the full onboarding flow to surface every friction point. The biggest drop-off moments: account creation (step 2) and soundscape setup (step 5).

4

User Survey

Surveyed 200 users about sleep habits and app expectations. 78% said they'd be more likely to sign up if they could try a sleep sound first.

Design Process

With a clear picture of where users dropped off, I restructured the onboarding flow around a single principle: let users experience the core feature before asking them to commit.

1

Reframe the Onboarding Flow

Moved soundscape selection to step 1, before account creation. Users now experience the app's signature feature within 30 seconds of opening.

2

Personalized Soundscape Selection

Designed an interactive soundscape picker that adapts recommendations based on the user's stated sleep goal — fall asleep faster, stay asleep, or wake up refreshed.

3

Sleep Insight Dashboard

Redesigned the home screen to lead with actionable insights rather than raw data — showing sleep trends in plain language with suggested next actions.

4

Paywall Timing Redesign

Repositioned the premium offer after 3+ nights of value, with contextual prompts tied to each user's specific usage pattern rather than a fixed trigger.

Key Design Decisions

Every decision was grounded in a specific research finding — here are the four that moved the needle most.

Value Before Commitment

Moved soundscape playback to step 1, before account creation. Users who heard a sound first converted at 2.4× the rate of those who didn't.

Progressive Disclosure

Broke the original 8-step onboarding into 3 phases, revealing features progressively. Users felt less overwhelmed and stayed engaged longer.

Social Proof at the Paywall

Added "X people improving sleep this week" and user testimonials at the premium moment. Trust signals reduced paywall abandonment significantly.

Contextual Premium Prompts

Replaced the generic paywall with usage-tied prompts: "You've used Rain sounds 5× — unlock 40+ more." Personalizing the value proposition lifted conversion.

Reflection & Next Steps

The biggest learning was the power of leading with experience rather than account creation. Users don't need to believe in your product — they need to feel it. This principle now informs how I approach every onboarding design challenge.

Next steps I'd pursue with more time: A/B test a personalized AI recommendation engine for soundscapes, explore Apple Watch integration for passive sleep data collection, and run a longitudinal study on 90-day retention after the redesign.

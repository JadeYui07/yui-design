EdTechLive — Personalized Instructional Strategies

Empowering students to share their voices and educators to build more engaging, effective learning experiences

The Problem

EdTechLive's platform, built around the SEEDS framework for personalized instruction, had an interface that wasn't enabling the behaviors it was designed to support. Students weren't using the voice-sharing tools, and educators were spending too much time navigating rather than teaching.

The core tool — student voice sharing — was buried three levels deep in navigation. Educators couldn't customize instruction without extensive manual effort, and the data visualizations meant to guide teaching decisions were too complex to act on.

Research & Discovery

Research spanned both sides of the classroom — students and educators — to understand where the platform was failing each group differently.

1

Heuristic Evaluation

Identified 12 usability violations in the existing platform — most critically: the navigation structure didn't reflect either the educator's or student's mental model of the workflow.

2

8+ Qualitative Interviews

Conducted in-depth interviews with teachers and students across grade levels. Key finding: educators wanted insights before each class, not after; students wanted to feel heard immediately.

3

Contextual Inquiry

Observed two live classroom sessions where EdTechLive was in use. Educators switched away from the platform 6+ times per session to manage tasks it should have handled natively.

4

Affinity Mapping & Synthesis

Synthesized findings into two role-based need maps — one for students, one for educators — revealing that the same feature needed entirely different UI treatments for each group.

Design Process

The redesign centered on a single structural decision: role-based dashboards. Students and educators needed fundamentally different starting points, each surfacing the right tools without any navigation overhead.

1

Redefine Information Architecture

Restructured the entire platform IA around user role and task frequency. Student Voice moved from level 3 navigation to the primary action on every student's home view.

2

Role-Based Dashboards

Designed separate entry experiences for students and educators — each showing only what's relevant before the next class, with the right level of data complexity for each role.

3

Simplified Data Visualization

Replaced dense data tables with plain-language insight cards — "Most students struggled with Q3 today. Consider revisiting tomorrow." Actionable, not just informational.

4

2+ Rounds of Usability Testing

Tested low-fidelity prototypes with real teachers and students. Each round uncovered specific interaction gaps that informed the next iteration before moving to high-fidelity.

Key Design Decisions

Four decisions that fundamentally changed how users experienced the platform.

Student Voice as Primary Action

Moved the core sharing feature to the top of the student view — always one tap away. Students who saw it immediately used it 3× more often in testing.

Plain-Language Insights

Replaced data tables with interpretive cards written in plain English. Educators reported significantly faster pre-class prep time in usability sessions.

Role-Based Entry Points

Students and educators log in to different home views. Eliminating irrelevant UI reduced cognitive load and task completion time for both groups.

Progressive Educator Onboarding

New educators encounter SEEDS features one at a time, contextually, as they build their first lesson — rather than a feature dump at first login.

Outcome & Metrics

This project was research- and quality-driven rather than conversion-focused. Success was measured through qualitative depth and rigorous usability validation.

Reflection & Next Steps

The most important shift was recognizing that the same feature needs completely different UI for different roles. A student sharing their voice and an educator reviewing that voice are using the same data in opposite directions — and the interface has to reflect that asymmetry.

Next steps: a quantitative study measuring student voice submission rates post-launch, mobile optimization for students on shared devices, and an expansion of the educator insight cards to surface cross-class patterns over time.
